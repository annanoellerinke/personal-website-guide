# Your own little corner of the internet

*A website for the things you're making. ✷*

Hi! I'm Anna. I co-founded Homebody Studios, write Le Index, and keep finding new things I want to make online.

I built **[rink-e.com](https://rink-e.com/)** to bring my writing, work, recommendations, and projects together in one playful place. Take a look around for a little inspiration :)

This guide will help you create a home for your own work, starting with your style, personality, and the things you want to share. Bring your Substack, ShopMy, business, products, courses, portfolio, or whatever else you're building. You don't need to have the whole vision figured out yet. That's what the first part is for.

---

## A few things to bring

- **Your links.** The exact URLs you want people to visit, plus a little context about each one.
- **Your inspiration.** Photos, screenshots, objects, spaces, colors, websites—anything that catches your eye. Tell your assistant what you like about it.
- **Your opinions.** What feels like you? What definitely doesn't? You can figure this out as you go.

## Let's make something

Open your coding assistant and paste the prompt below. It will start with a few questions, help you explore different creative directions, and build a preview after you've chosen one.

You can give feedback exactly how you'd say it out loud. “This feels too serious” or “I want people to notice my writing first” is plenty to work with.

### Copy & paste this prompt ↓

Use the copy button in the top-right corner of the box, or select the text inside it.

```text
I want to build a personal website that brings my links and work together in a way
that feels unmistakably like me. Help me discover the concept, then help me build
it.

Use this guide and supporting code:
https://github.com/annanoellerinke/personal-website-guide

Start by getting to know me. Ask a few thoughtful questions at a time about what I
make, what I care about, who the site is for, and what I want people to do when they
arrive. My work might include writing, a Substack, ShopMy, a business, products,
courses, services, projects, a portfolio, events, a community, social accounts, or
something else entirely. Help me figure out what belongs here and what deserves the
most attention.

Ask me to share inspiration: photos, screenshots, mood boards, objects, spaces,
packaging, art, websites, colors, textures, typography, and anything that feels like
my world. Ask what I like about each reference and what I don't want. If I don't
know my aesthetic yet, help me find it through specific choices and conversation.
Don't make me arrive with a polished creative brief.

Use what you learn to propose three genuinely different directions. Give each one a
name and explain its visual idea, layout, typography, colors, materials, and how
someone would explore it on a computer and phone. Tie the choices to things I've
actually told or shown you. These should differ in concept and experience, not just
color. The site can be playful, editorial, atmospheric, tactile, minimal, or
something unexpected. Don't default to a familiar template or assume it needs to
resemble a physical object. Draw inspiration from references without reproducing
their composition, branding, or distinctive assets.

Help me choose or combine directions, then show me a small visual study before
building the full website. Wait for my approval of the direction. I want something
personal and memorable that is also clear and easy to use.

Collect every exact destination URL from me, along with the label, a short
explanation, and which links matter most. Keep inspiration links separate from the
links visitors will click. Never invent a URL, substitute a product page for my
affiliate link, or guess a bio, testimonial, metric, or offering. Flag missing
information and keep unfinished links out of the published site.

Once we've agreed on the direction, create the files and build a working preview.
Use the starter's content tools where useful; the visual design is yours to create
with me. Make interactions purposeful, navigation obvious, text readable, and the
mobile experience thoughtful. Make sure touch, keyboard navigation, and
reduced-motion preferences work too.

Let me refine the preview in ordinary language. Explain technical decisions simply
and handle the code for me. Before publishing, verify the links and real
functionality, show me the finished result, and help me choose hosting and connect
my own domain if I have one. Ask before making it public or adding anything that
costs money.

Start with three questions that will help you understand me—not a long
questionnaire.
```

---

**Once it feels right:** ask your assistant to check every link, try it on a phone, and help you put it online. If you own a domain, tell it. You can make those decisions together when you're ready.
