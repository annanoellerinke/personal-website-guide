I want to build a personal website that brings my links and work together in a way that feels unmistakably like me. Help me discover the concept, then help me build it.

Use this guide and supporting code: https://github.com/annanoellerinke/personal-website-guide

Start by getting to know me. Ask a few thoughtful questions at a time about what I make, what I care about, who the site is for, and what I want people to do when they arrive. My work might include writing, a Substack, ShopMy, a business, products, courses, services, projects, a portfolio, events, a community, social accounts, or something else entirely. Help me figure out what belongs here and what deserves the most attention.

Ask me to share inspiration: photos, screenshots, mood boards, objects, spaces, packaging, art, websites, colors, textures, typography, and anything that feels like my world. Ask what I like about each reference and what I don't want. If I don't know my aesthetic yet, help me find it through specific choices and conversation. Don't make me arrive with a polished creative brief.

Use what you learn to propose three genuinely different directions. Give each one a name and explain its visual idea, layout, typography, colors, materials, and how someone would explore it on a computer and phone. Tie the choices to things I've actually told or shown you. These should differ in concept and experience, not just color. The site can be playful, editorial, atmospheric, tactile, minimal, or something unexpected. Don't default to a familiar template or assume it needs to resemble a physical object. Draw inspiration from references without reproducing their composition, branding, or distinctive assets.

Help me choose or combine directions, then show me a small visual study before building the full website. Wait for my approval of the direction. I want something personal and memorable that is also clear and easy to use.

Collect every exact destination URL from me, along with the label, a short explanation, and which links matter most. Keep inspiration links separate from the links visitors will click. Never invent a URL, substitute a product page for my affiliate link, or guess a bio, testimonial, metric, or offering. Flag missing information and keep unfinished links out of the published site.

Once we've agreed on the direction, create the files and build a working preview. Use the starter's content tools where useful; the visual design is yours to create with me. Make interactions purposeful, navigation obvious, text readable, and the mobile experience thoughtful. Make sure touch, keyboard navigation, and reduced-motion preferences work too.

Let me refine the preview in ordinary language. Explain technical decisions simply and handle the code for me. Before publishing, verify the links and real functionality, show me the finished result, and help me choose hosting and connect my own domain if I have one. Ask before making it public or adding anything that costs money.

Start with three questions that will help you understand me—not a long questionnaire.
