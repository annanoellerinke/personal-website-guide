# Things you can bring

You don't have to fill this out. Your assistant can gather the details through conversation and keep track of them for you.

## Your world

What do you make or do? What are you excited about right now? Who is the site for? What do you want someone to understand or do after visiting? What should they discover first?

What feels like you outside your work—a place, collection, habit, object, book, room, film, piece of clothing, or way of writing? These can be clues rather than literal things to put on the page.

## Your eye

Share a few pictures, screenshots, or links. Tell your assistant what caught your eye in each one. “The tiny labels,” “the way the light hits the wood,” or “how quiet it feels” is more useful than just “I like this.”

Include something you don't like if that makes your taste easier to describe. You can like the color of one reference and the organization of another.

You can include writing, recommendations, businesses, products, courses, services, projects, portfolios, events, communities, social accounts, or other work. You don't need one of everything. The best organization depends on what you actually want to share.

## Your words and images

Bring a short bio, existing copy that sounds like you, your name as it should appear, photos or illustrations you can use, and a logo if you have one. It is fine to have none of these ready—the assistant can help draft copy from facts you provide and propose original visuals.

## Practical details, later

Once the concept feels right, decide how often you expect to update it, whether you need an actual form or just a contact link, and whether you own a domain. If a feature requires a service or paid plan, the assistant should explain that before connecting it. Don't paste passwords or API keys into the prompt or public files.
